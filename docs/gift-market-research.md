## 2. Sri Lanka — Sinhala and Tamil homes

- **Birthdays:** the day often starts with *kiribath* (milk rice) and a temple, kovil, church or mosque visit, then cake cutting. Ribbon cake and chocolate biscuit pudding are favourites, and milestone birthdays get themed or photo cakes.
- **Sinhala & Tamil New Year (Avurudu / Puthandu, April):** gifts are exchanged at the auspicious time (*ganu denu*), and children receive gifts and money from elders. Families make *kavum, kokis, athirasa, aluwa* and share them with neighbours. Visiting relatives is expected, and bringing sweets or fruit is welcome.
- **Housewarming:** Sinhala families boil milk until it overflows (*kiri ithirima*) at an auspicious time. Oil lamps, plants and home items are typical gifts.
- **Weddings:** the Poruwa ceremony and homecoming. Cash in decorated envelopes, keepsakes and wedding or homecoming cakes.
- **Thai Pongal (January, Tamil):** pongal is boiled in a new clay pot. New clothes, sugarcane and sweets are shared.
- **Christmas:** Sri Lankan Christmas cake and love cake are shared across communities.

## 3. India and the Indian diaspora

- **Griha Pravesh (housewarming):** puja, lit lamp and kolam. Gifts are often opened in front of guests. Lakshmi and Ganesha idols, brass lamps and home items are common choices.
- **Wrapping colours:** many families avoid white or black for happy occasions and prefer red, yellow or green.
- **Weddings:** cash in a *shagun* envelope with the giver's name written on it.
- **Diwali:** mithai (sweets), dry fruits, diyas and décor. Idols or religious books suit spiritually-minded friends.
- **Return gifts:** sweets or small useful items for guests at functions.

## 4. Muslim and other communities

- **Eid:** dates, sweets, attar and new clothes, and money (*Eidi*) for children. Choose halal and avoid alcohol-related items.
- Ask about the recipient's religious background so the gift doesn't cause offence by accident.
